The Adoption Gap

Approval is not adoption

In neurorehabilitation, earning a UKCA or CE mark should be the beginning of a device's clinical life. Too often it's where progress stalls — proven technology stranded on the wrong side of a fragmented, risk-averse system while patients lose the recovery windows that matter most.

The problem

The valley between proof and patients

A manufacturer can spend years and millions earning regulatory approval, then hit a second, harder barrier: fragmented procurement across hundreds of NHS bodies, institutional caution toward unproven deployments, and payers who won't commission without real-world evidence — evidence that can only be generated through the very adoption they're withholding.

The result is a market where internationally proven innovations spread slowly and unevenly, and patients bear the cost through lost neuroplasticity windows and unaffordable private alternatives.

The Verada model

An ecosystem that closes it

Rather than bridge each barrier one device at a time, Verada removes them structurally. The group brings a proven technology through UK regulatory entry, deploys it into its own care delivery, and generates the UK-specific clinical, operational, and economic evidence that public and private systems need to adopt at scale.

Regulatory navigation, real-world evidence, and market access combine in a single operating model — cutting the time and risk for rehabilitation innovation to reach the people it was built for.

The Compounding Advantage

Each layer makes the next one stronger

The group's defensibility isn't in any single layer — it's in the interlock. Delivering care generates exclusive, longitudinal rehabilitation data. That data sharpens the models and clinical protocols. Better outcomes win the trust of payers and open channel access. And that access drives more delivery.

Every turn of the loop deepens all of it at once — data that can't be acquired retrospectively, evidence in the formats commissioners already trust, and a route to market that gets harder to replicate with each patient treated.

The moat widens with volume rather than eroding under competition — because the advantage is built from the care itself.

A Repeatable Pathway

Built once, reused for every technology

The hard work is done once and amortised across the whole portfolio. Each new neurorehabilitation technology follows the same three-part route — reusing the regulatory foundations, clinical infrastructure, and evidence engine already in place.

1

Regulatory alignment

Bring a proven technology through UK regulatory entry — reusing established UKCA/CE foundations, responsible-person structures, and MHRA pathways rather than rebuilding them for each device.

2

Evidence generation

Deploy it into real patient pathways within Verada's own care delivery, capturing the UK-specific clinical, operational, and economic outcomes that systems require before they adopt.

3

Scaled adoption

Convert that evidence into market access — beginning with direct care, then private medical insurer pathways, and ultimately NHS commissioning at population scale.
