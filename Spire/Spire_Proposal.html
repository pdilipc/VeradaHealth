The problem

Deterioration hides between the observations.

On a non-critical ward vital signs are checked at intervals and recorded by hand. The changes that precede a serious event develop in the gap between those checks, and that gap is widest overnight and at weekends.

Occult deterioration

Vital-sign derangement often precedes symptoms by hours and is not captured by intermittent observation.

Delayed recognition

Later recognition produces later escalation, which is associated with worse outcomes.

Weakest when it matters

Surveillance tied to bedside staffing is least robust overnight and during reduced staffing.

The science

Contactless by design, beneath the mattress.

A thin sensor sits under the mattress and reads the tiny movements of each heartbeat and breath. The raw signal is resolved into respiratory and cardiac components, the basis of ballistocardiography.

Acquired parameters

Continuous physiology, plus accessories where indicated.

ContactlessHeart rate. Validated to within a few beats per minute.
ContactlessRespiratory rate. Often the earliest parameter to deviate.
ContactlessHRV, occupancy and gross movement.
ContactlessBlood-pressure trend. A surveillance indicator, not a diagnostic measurement.
AccessorySpO2, cuff NIBP, temperature where clinically indicated.
AccessorySingle-lead ECG for rhythm and AF screening.

These are the physiological components of an early-warning score and do not constitute a complete validated NEWS2. Intended scope is non-critical: adult general wards, post-operative and step-down beds.

Post-operative sepsis

Signal
Recurrent tachycardia over hours, visible as a trend.
Review
Command-centre clinician recognises the sustained rise.
Response
Ward team investigates and treats earlier.

Why it matters: the same trend is often not apparent on intermittent observation until the patient is overtly unwell.

Fluid overload

Signal
Respiratory rate rises, tracking fluid status.
Review
Trend recognised and communicated with data.
Response
Clinician orders a diuretic; trend followed as it settles.

Why it matters: continuous trending surfaces the problem earlier and measures the response. The decision stays with the clinician.
