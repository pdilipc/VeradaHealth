For operational leadership

Continuous monitoring run by our team, not more equipment for yours to manage

We monitor every bed in the service continuously from our command centre, and we contact your ward team only when a patient needs attention, with a signal one of our clinicians has already reviewed. Your staff are not asked to watch another screen or learn another system. The aim is to reduce the monitoring workload on the ward, not add to it.

What changes on the ward

Monitoring that does not depend on the roster

Monitoring done by ward staff is only as consistent as the staffing on a given shift. Our command centre watches to the same standard at every hour, which matters most overnight, at weekends and when the ward is short-staffed.

Less time spent on manual observations

Continuous monitoring replaces a share of the manual observation rounds and hand-written charting. Comparable sites report around one hour per nurse per shift returned to direct patient care.

A site without on-site critical care sometimes has to stabilise and hold an acutely unwell patient until an HDU or ITU bed, or an ambulance, is available. We keep that patient under continuous, clinician-reviewed monitoring throughout the wait and flag any further change immediately.

One managed service across your non-critical beds, with the devices provided and supported as part of it. There is nothing for your teams to build, staff or maintain.

For the Medical Director and clinical governance

Physiological deterioration is frequently occult between intermittent observations

In the non-critical inpatient setting, vital-sign surveillance is predominantly intermittent: observations are performed at scheduled intervals and recorded manually. The physiological antecedents to a serious adverse event commonly develop in the interval between observations. On the overnight ward or in the post-operative bay this period is frequently unobserved, and these are the settings in which continuous monitoring is least often established.

Occult antecedent deterioration

Derangement in vital signs frequently precedes symptomatic presentation by several hours and is not captured by intermittent observation.

Delay in the afferent limb

Later recognition produces later escalation. Delay in the recognition-and-response pathway is associated with increased in-hospital mortality.

Burden on the general ward

The majority of in-hospital cardiac arrests occur on general wards rather than in critical care, and a substantial proportion are considered potentially preventable.

The stabilise-and-transfer interval

A facility without on-site HDU or ITU must stabilise and safely hold an acutely unwell patient pending escalation or inter-hospital transfer. This interval, which can be prolonged by critical-care bed and ambulance availability, is a defined period in which close and continuous observation is clinically important.

Variation with staffing

Surveillance contingent on bedside staffing varies with the roster and is least robust overnight, at weekends and during reduced staffing.

Acquired physiological parameters

ContactlessHeart rate. Validated against electrocardiographic reference to a mean absolute error of the order of a few beats per minute.
ContactlessRespiratory rate. Frequently the earliest parameter to deviate in evolving respiratory and septic compromise.
ContactlessHeart-rate variability, bed occupancy and gross movement.
ContactlessBlood-pressure trend. A trend indicator for surveillance, not a diagnostic blood-pressure measurement.
AccessorySpO₂, cuff NIBP and temperature where clinically indicated.
AccessorySingle-lead ECG patch for rhythm assessment and atrial-fibrillation screening.

The scored parameters represent the physiological components of an early-warning score. They do not constitute a complete validated NEWS2, which additionally requires level of consciousness and inspired-oxygen status. The intended scope is non-critical: adult inpatients on general wards and in post-operative and step-down beds, including the interval in which an acutely unwell patient is stabilised and held pending escalation or transfer. The service supports surveillance during that interval; it does not provide critical-care treatment, and it does not alter the availability of critical-care beds or inter-hospital transport.

Illustrative pathways

How continuous monitoring changes the recognition window

Illustrative examples, not Verada case data

The following are constructed examples of the mechanism of benefit on two physiologically-expressed pathways. They are not Verada patients or Verada outcomes. Verada holds no clinical track record in the United Kingdom; any effect would be measured prospectively in an agreed evaluation.

Post-operative sepsis: recognition at the trend stage

Signal
Recurrent tachycardia develops over several hours and is visible as a continuous trend.
Review
The Command Centre clinician recognises a sustained, unexplained rise and communicates it to the ward.
Response
The ward team examines the patient, investigates, and begins treatment earlier in the course.
Outcome
A developing infection is addressed before it reaches an emergency, within the facility.

Why it matters: on intermittent observation the same trend is frequently not apparent until a scheduled round or until the patient is overtly unwell. Continuous monitoring makes the trajectory visible earlier. In a UK setting the value is in avoided deterioration and avoided emergency transfer, not a reimbursement claim.

Fluid overload: a measured response, titrated to the signal

Signal
Respiratory rate rises steadily, tracking fluid status before overt respiratory distress.
Review
The trend is recognised and communicated with the supporting data.
Response
The responsible clinician orders a diuretic; the respiratory-rate trend is followed as it settles.
Outcome
The episode is managed within the facility, with the response guided by the continuous signal.

Why it matters: continuous respiratory-rate trending both surfaces the problem earlier and provides an objective measure of the response to treatment. The prescribing decision remains with the responsible clinician throughout.

Clinical governance and accountability

A surveillance layer that supports Spire's clinical teams rather than supplanting them

Spire remains the registered provider and retains the duty of care for its patients. Alerts function as decision support and are adjudicated by a registered clinician before communication to the ward. No clinical decision is automated, and no unreviewed data is passed to ward staff for interpretation. The division of clinical responsibility is defined jointly and documented, consistent with the governance standards Verada is establishing for CQC registration.

For the Finance Director

A managed cost you can size and control, set against the cost of late deterioration

The service is bought as an operating cost, not a capital project. There is no monitoring estate to buy, build or depreciate: the devices and the command centre come as one managed service, priced per monitored bed. The case for it rests on cost you already carry when a patient deteriorates late.

Operating cost, not capital

A predictable per-bed service charge in place of the fixed overhead of building and staffing an equivalent capability in house. You scope it, and you can scale it up or down.

Protects your most expensive beds

Earlier recognition and timely step-down reduce avoidable escalations into high-dependency and intensive-care beds, which are the costliest to run in a private facility.

Lowers the cost of the event

A complication caught as a trend is, on average, cheaper to manage than one caught late, in both length of stay and intensity of care.

Returns clinical time

Reviewed signals replace a share of manual observation and charting, returning nursing time to direct care rather than to watching data.

The shape of the case

  • Avoided escalations. Each avoided unplanned admission to HDU or ICU is a direct saving against the highest bed-day cost in the hospital.
  • Avoided length of stay. Earlier intervention shortens the deterioration episodes that otherwise extend a stay.
  • Staffing efficiency. Time returned to the bedside reduces reliance on additional or agency cover at the margin.
  • Medico-legal exposure. A documented, time-stamped record of continuous monitoring and defined escalation supports the hospital's position if an outcome is later questioned.

The service commits to service levels (surveillance cover, signal-review and escalation turnaround, uptime and reporting), not to clinical outcomes. The financial case would be built with Spire against its own cost base and measured over an agreed pilot, rather than asserted here.

For information governance, the DPO, Caldicott and assurance

Data protection, clinical safety and assurance, defined before any patient is monitored

The service processes special-category health data and provides a clinical surveillance function. Both carry defined obligations. Verada's position is to agree the data-protection and clinical-safety arrangements with Spire in writing before any patient is monitored, and to keep them under joint review.

Data protection

Processing is governed by UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018. A Data Protection Impact Assessment is completed for the deployment, with lawful basis, roles and data flows defined with Spire, and a data-processing agreement in place. UK data residency is confirmed before go-live.

Information security

The underlying monitoring platform holds ISO/IEC 27001 and SOC 2 Type II. Alignment with the Data Security and Protection Toolkit at the service layer is confirmed as part of onboarding.

Clinical safety

Clinical-risk management follows the DCB0129 and DCB0160 standards: the manufacturer's clinical-safety case for the device, and Verada's own clinical-safety case for the monitoring and escalation service, each with a named Clinical Safety Officer and a maintained hazard log.

Device regulatory status

The monitoring technology is a CE-marked medical device recognised for the Great Britain market. Any regulatory approvals are held by the manufacturer. The specific device, its GB conformity and Verada's CQC registration are confirmed before any service is provided.
