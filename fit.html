Who it's for

Is this you?

We are not useful to everyone, and a measurement partner who claims otherwise is worth less than one who tells you where they stop. This page is the test we apply before we take a conversation any further, published so you can apply it yourself.

Two Doors

Where your technology is decides what we can do

The single most important fact about a technology is not what it does. It is whether it has been approved yet, because that determines which evidence is still collectable and which has already closed.

Door one

Approved, and going nowhere

A CE, UKCA or FDA-cleared technology that works in other health systems and has no meaningful UK or US footprint. The science is settled. The barrier is that nobody will buy what nobody has run.

  • What you need: a named reference site, operational and economic proof, a deployment blueprint, and clinicians who will speak to their peers
  • What we do: adopt it into our own care delivery at our commercial risk, and publish what it actually takes to run
  • What you cannot do later: nothing is lost by waiting, but every quarter without a reference site is a quarter of adoption you do not get back

Door two

Investigational, protocol still open

A device, drug, biologic or advanced therapy in development, where the endpoint architecture has not yet locked and the measurement burden falls on participants who have to travel to be assessed.

  • What you need: decentralised delivery, objective endpoints, and signal dense enough to see a trajectory rather than two timepoints
  • What we do: instrumented delivery in the participant's own environment, with all four outcome-assessment types on one record
  • What you cannot do later: randomisation, a clean baseline, a blinded assessment and a pre-specified endpoint cannot be reconstructed afterwards at any price

Most technologies pass through both doors. Enter in trials, continue through approval into routine care, and the record runs unbroken from first baseline to a commissioning decision. That continuity is why both divisions exist.
The Test

Three conditions, and all of them have to hold

This is the filter we run before proposing anything. It exists to stop the fit map becoming a list of every neurological indication, which is what it would otherwise be.

1Measurable axis
The condition, or its treatment, expresses in cortical state, neuromuscular control, or the coupling between them. That is the specific physiological layer our instrumentation reads. For musculoskeletal and orthopaedic work the axis is muscle activation and range of motion, and the instrument is different.
2Weak incumbent
The endpoints currently in use are subjective, rater-dependent or episodic. If your existing measure is already objective and well validated, we are competing rather than filling a gap, and we will say so.
3Signal sensitivity
The signal plausibly moves with the intervention's effect, not merely with its presence. This is the condition most often assumed and least often true. A psychoactive dose will change an EEG whether or not the participant improves, so demonstrating target engagement is easy and demonstrating an efficacy correlate is the real work.
Neurological-sounding is not the same as brain and muscle measurable. Conflating the two is the fastest way a measurement proposal loses a methodologist.
Strongest Fit

Where the axis we measure is the axis you are treating

In these categories the instrument is not a proxy. It reads the layer the intervention acts on, which means it can answer the question the trial is actually asking rather than inferring it from an observed movement.

Strongest

Neuromodulation & rehabilitation devices

Brain-computer interfaces, functional electrical stimulation, robotic rehabilitation, transcranial stimulation, paired neurostimulation, neurofeedback. These act directly on cortical excitability and brain-muscle coupling. Cleanest mechanistic fit on the map.

Strongest

Cell, gene & advanced therapies

Neuro-restoration in stroke, TBI and neurodegeneration. Large functional-restoration claims on very small participant numbers, graded on the motor scales that drift most. Rater noise a large trial averages out is fatal to a forty-patient study.

Strongest

Recovery & antispasticity pharmacology

Plasticity-enhancing agents, botulinum toxin and antispasticity drugs. These are dosed precisely to change agonist-antagonist balance and co-contraction, which is exactly what the instrument quantifies. The measurement is of the intended effect, not a downstream proxy.

Strong On A Secondary Axis

Where we measure something real, but not your primary target

Here the fit is genuine and the honest framing differs by category. Two of these compete with tools that already exist, and we would rather say so than discover it in a methods discussion.

Strong

Antipsychotics & psychiatry

Drug-induced parkinsonism, akathisia and tardive dyskinesia. The motor signal moves adversely with the drug, which is precisely why it is measurable. The vulnerability in current scales is that they are episodic and clinic-based, not that raters are incompetent.

Strong

Cognition-targeting CNS drugs

Schizophrenia cognition, mild cognitive impairment and dementia assets. Cognitive batteries carry well-documented ceiling and practice effects and systematically omit the motor dimension. We supply what the battery misses rather than replacing it.

Competes

Movement disorders

Validated motor scales and actigraphy already exist here and are well established. Claiming superiority as a motor quantifier would invite immediate and deserved pushback. The defensible wedge is cognitive-motor coupling and between-visit home capture, which the incumbents do not address.

Partial

Multiple sclerosis

The functional and cognitive-fatigue layer only. Disability scales capture that layer coarsely and fatigue is under-instrumented relative to its impact on daily life. We say nothing about the immunological disease activity the drug actually modifies.

Strong

Oncology supportive care & survivorship

We cannot measure a tumour. We can often measure what treating it leaves behind: chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy, pelvic floor dysfunction after prostate surgery or radiotherapy, shoulder restriction after axillary surgery, radiation fibrosis, and treatment-related deconditioning. Currently graded on coarse clinician scales and patient recall.

Separate instrument

Musculoskeletal & orthobiologics

Regenerative and injectable interventions, post-surgical and conservative recovery, sports medicine. Real fit, different device: muscle activation with range of motion rather than the brain-muscle stack. A different sponsor community and a separate conversation.

Where We Do Not Fit

Where we say no

Every measurement vendor claims to serve everyone. Ours is a narrow instrument, and the categories below fail the test above for reasons that will not change.

The exclusions are about mechanism, not therapy area. Asking "do you cover oncology?" is the wrong question. The right one is "which axis is the intervention acting on?" We cannot measure a tumour. We can often measure the functional damage caused by treating it, which is a different and frequently under-instrumented endpoint.

  • No fitTumour biology as the endpoint. Response, progression and survival are not on any axis we read.
  • No fitImmunological targets, including MS disease activity. The drug acts where the signal cannot reach. Fails condition three.
  • No fitPeripheral pain as a primary endpoint. Neurological-sounding, but the mechanism is not on our axis.
  • No fitSeizure and epilepsy therapeutics. A different EEG question requiring different instrumentation and interpretation. It is also a safety exclusion for our own devices.
  • No fitAnything where the signal is present in the population but does not move with treatment. The population being neurological is not sufficient.

We also maintain internal exclusions on ethical rather than commercial grounds, where an application would require consent from people whose capacity to give it is part of the condition being treated. Those are not sized, they are declined.

A partner who has never turned anything down has not told you anything by accepting you.
Getting To An Answer

What we would ask you first

Five questions settle most of it, and none of them require you to share anything confidential. If the answers point the wrong way we will tell you in the first conversation rather than the fourth.

Stage
Where is the asset? Pre-clinical, early phase, pivotal, approved but not scaled, or in routine use. This decides which door you are at and how much of the evidence architecture is still open.
Mechanism
Does the intervention act on cortical state, neuromuscular control, or the coupling between them? If the honest answer is no, condition three fails and nothing else matters.
Current endpoint
What are you measuring today, and is its weakness a recognised risk in your programme or a theoretical one? Sponsors who have already felt an ambiguous readout need no persuading.
Participant burden
How far do participants travel, how often, and what is your retention doing? Decentralised delivery is worth most where site visits are the binding constraint.
Timeline
When does the protocol lock, or when do you need a UK presence? Some of what we offer cannot be retrofitted after those dates.

Think you are on the list?

Tell us the stage, the mechanism and the measure you are using now. If we are not a fit we will say so quickly, which is worth more to both of us than a longer conversation.
